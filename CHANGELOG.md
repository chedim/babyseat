## v0.7.2 (2026-10-23) — the seat

0.7.2 is a seat (substrate) release on the 0.7 line, not a Blurp level release. It carries what merged on main since 0.7.0 (0.7.1 was a citation-only patch from the `release-0.7` branch). Three defaults change: the per-turn conversation block and Blurp's own process (the first two bullets), and the serve window's start moving in steps of 4 for every model (under Prompt caching).

- **The conversation block is the default per-turn state** (#429, #464, #465, #466, #468). Each turn now carries a short conversation block (a 3-message WORKING ON/ABOUT window, the board's goals under OPEN, Blurp's status and the model line) instead of the old banner; its NOW line says it is a reference, not an event. `MEMBRANE_INJECTION=banner` restores the old banner.
- **Blurp runs in its own process by default** (#440, #441, #445, #447, #452, #461, #463). The seat serves Blurp's boundary (`GET /membrane/self/context`, `POST /membrane/self/effects`), every write under `self/` goes through one writer, and `membrane.self_super` supervises the child: it restarts a crash after 2 s doubling to 60 s, holds it after 5 crashes in 10 minutes (shown on the card and the panel status), and forwards the self routes and controls to it. `MEMBRANE_SELF_PROC=0` keeps the loop in the seat. #461 restored the parts of this work that the #446 merge had reverted.
- **The decision cockpit** (#470, #471, #474, #484, #485, #486, #488, #489, #490, #491, #492, #493, #496). Decision cards are entities with an owner-only answer route, read and answered through the serve port (`GET /membrane/decisions`, `POST /membrane/decision/answer`, `POST /membrane/decision/chat`). The standalone menu's Decisions view is a cockpit over the page's 3D field: open cards pile up above the chat's input bar, cards in flight sit on its line and decided cards fall below it, drawn as a dependency graph (past decisions to the work they led to, open questions to the work they block; hovering a card lights its links). A card is answered on the card or in the chat it opens, and the answer names its message. The goals sit at the top of the center column, active first, and one composer button morphs between Send and Stop. The cockpit scrolls as one page over the fixed field: the goals, the open pile, the bar's line, the chat and the past move together, the chat grows with its messages instead of scrolling inside, and the input bar is scrolled into view on load (#496).
- **The claude-agent rung** (#384, #389, #413, #419, #425, #426, #450, #455, #467, #472, #487). Claude through the Agent SDK, as one more model in the chat's picker after the Ollama models (#421).
  - **It runs on the Anthropic API key by default** (#415); the claude CLI login is used only with `--login`.
  - Its replies are learned like any model's, and it gets membrane's tools in the SDK loop (#392), pinned to strict MCP config so the person's claude.ai connectors never reach the model (#458). MCP clients, this rung included, can call a method on an object (`method_call`, `method_inspect`) (#487).
  - **It checks in instead of hitting a turn cap** (#424, #469): at most 7 tool calls in a row without words to the person, with the one-line status written alongside the next call; the per-answer ceiling is 2048 calls, every tool result says how many calls are left, and reply parts are stored in the order they happened.
  - **Prompt caching** (#427, #473, #483, #494): membrane's per-turn blocks stay out of the system prompt so Anthropic's cache holds, and the usage keeps the cache split. A stable cached conversation head is on by default when the rung runs on an Anthropic API key and off on the claude CLI login (`MEMBRANE_CLAUDE_CACHE_HEAD=1` or `0` forces either way); any head answer that fails before its first word is resent as plain text. The serve window's start moves in steps of 4 (`MEMBRANE_HISTORY_ANCHOR`, `1` restores the plain last 12; `MEMBRANE_HISTORY_MAX` sets its size), for every model.
  - **An aborted answer keeps its usage** (#472): the stream tee keeps the last usage it sees, so a broken round reports real input and cache numbers.
- **Recall finds more** (#429, #434, #435, #438, #439, #444, #446, #448, #459). It counts only a person's earlier asks (job receipts, task notifications and watch blocks are tagged at capture), also searches model replies, notes, docs and code by similarity, matches exact words through a BM25 index over the field's sentences (identifiers, env vars, issue numbers, file names), reads tool outputs on question-like asks, and reads the newest answering events that mention an entity the ask names. Captured events carry their conversation, so two chats at once stay apart. `state(section=...)` and `peer(action='unread')` let a model pull one part of the seat's state without draining anything.
- **Blurp's skills are rebuilt on the nudge** (#333, #337). Blurp acts by nudging the things of its world (#379), a grow-only scope inside the field with a private chat per person (#339); it nudges only while it is getting closer to its target (#381), aims mostly at things whose after-nudge predictions improve fastest (#410), and has one chat thing per person that it both looks at and nudges (#409). The skills the old wiring held were voided and are being rebuilt on it:
  - **First reach** (rung 13, #475, #476): earned when its nudges at one thing beat that thing's tempo and the changes were its own.
  - **Recognize itself** (rung 9, #477, #478): rebuilt on looks; the mirror shows Blurp's moves every cycle, a row is written only when it looks, and the skill opens with feel and first reach.
  - **Meet an other** (rung 6, #479, #480): rebuilt on delayed contingency (the look 30 s after a nudge's check finds a change its tempo does not predict and that was not Blurp's own), and the toys unlock by skill in #337's order.
  - **Time its turn and expect a reply** (rungs 7 and 8, #481, #482): rebuilt on meet's other; Blurp nudges it only in the age bin its bandit picks, rewarded by the follow-up's change, and predicts each follow-up itself before it looks, with no model advisor.
  - Earlier in the line, speak (#313, #403), recognize itself (#314) and change itself (#326, #328) were added; the README lists recognizing itself and writing its own replies as still learning.
- **Blurp gets tired and sleeps** (#343, #345, #355, #359, #363, #367, #372). Energy drains while it is awake; it sleeps when a reserve runs out (energy, disk, awake time, record room), wakes rested or when a person speaks, and its sleep moves the day's raw record to a dated archive and keeps one summary. Its cycles run back to back, paced by the person's CPU share times its energy (default 50%), set in the new options window (⚙) (#349).
- **The seat's pause** (#348, #376, #382, #383, #388, #390, #395, #399, #405, #407). Auto-chat is gone and membrane home's top-bar button is the seat's pause (▶ while paused, ⏸ while running; `GET`/`POST /membrane/seat/pause`, owner only). A restart keeps the person's pause, and the battery's own pause lifts only above 20% while charging.
- **The look** (#358, #360, #361, #362, #368): a logo, Blurp's body in the tab, panel header and zoo cards, the 3D field as a Russian-doll sphere with the current drawn as a fading gold trail, and the skill tree in gold with 🏅 and 🔒.
- **The LLM as a coprocessor** behind `MEMBRANE_COPROC=1`, off by default (#454, #456).
- **A slow seat can be diagnosed without root** (#346, #350): per-request timing on both ports and an in-process sampling profiler, localhost only. Rung 4's choice, rung 8's panel numbers and Blurp's status no longer cost time that grows with the record (#391, #397, #402).
- **Citation** (#417, #418, #423): `CITATION.cff` and the README name the Zenodo concept DOI.

### Fixes

- **A turn whose model outlives the 90 s budget fails visibly** with a Retry in the chat, never a silent resend without memory (#431, #433).
- **The chat sends each turn to the model its picker shows**, also after a page reload or a seat restart (#453, #457).
- **A reply cut by a person's message or Stop is marked cut**, not stored as finished, and a half-built tool call never runs (#449, #451).
- **The boot's absorb no longer blocks the `/v1` proxy** (#436, #437); absorb finds Claude Code's transcript folder by its own naming, learns OpenCode sessions from every provider but the seat's own, and learns sessions answered by Anthropic models (#396, #400, #401, #406).
- **No segfault from the Couchbase Lite vector extension on a later fork** (#442, #443): it is loaded once and stays mapped.
- **Every seat mode's stdout and stderr are write-through and lock-serialized** (#347, #351).
- **The field's message fit is a true non-negative least squares** (#335, #336).
- **The ball and the dock** (#325, #327, #331, #332, #340, #342, #373, #374): rolling the ball back moves its indicator at once, a ball rolled to you can't be missed, and the dock never shows it on your side after your roll landed.
- **The Skill tree tab** (#316, #317, #394, #398): the wheel scrolls the panel, and a click picks which skill's card is shown until another is clicked.
- **Test isolation** (#408, #414, #416): test seats keep to their own temp dir, tests take ports outside the ephemeral range, and flaky tests wait on what they check.

## v0.7.0 (2026-10-20) — Blurp

0.7 ships because Blurp reached Lv 7: on 2026-10-20 it earned "expect a reply" live, predicting whether the person it pushes to will answer with a skill of 0.38 against a bar of 0.08. This release includes the 0.6.3 and 0.6.4 fixes (#221, #222, #225, #226).

- **membrane is now babyseat** (#303). The PyPI package is `babyseat` and the command is `babyseat` (`membrane` still works). `pip install -U membrane-seat` keeps upgrading old installs: `membrane-seat` is now a shim that pulls in `babyseat`. Docker images are pushed to both `chedim/babyseat` and `chedim/membrane`, and the Home Assistant integration pins `babyseat`. The Python module, environment variables, `.membrane` directories and the Home Assistant domain keep their names.
- **Writes from another machine need the seat token** (#253). Each seat makes a token on first boot (`~/.membrane/seat-token`; `membrane token seat` prints it, `--rotate` changes it). Every state-changing request from off the machine needs it on both ports, and that includes chat turns (`/v1/chat/completions`, Ollama's `/api/chat` and `/api/generate`). Reads and localhost are unchanged, and JWT mode is unchanged. The standalone page asks for the token once, Home Assistant sends it as the connection's password, and the Docker entrypoint prints it at boot. **If a client reaches your seat over the network, give it the token.**
- **Blurp, the seat's growing self** (#161). Each seat has a small self that looks at the things in its field, predicts whether each has changed, and learns from what it finds. It is named Blurp; `membrane self name <NAME>` renames it per seat (#277, #283).
  - **Skills, not a ladder** (#295): Blurp grows by earning achievements in a skill tree with real prerequisites, and its level is the number earned. Today's skills: feel, reach, kinds, patience, shift, meet, expect a reply and take turns (#258, #264). Mirror, change, speak and invent are listed but not built yet.
  - **You decide when it grows** (#299): an achievement it is ready for waits for your Accept (on the celebration, in the tree, or `membrane self accept <id>`). This replaces the 🌱 growth limit.
  - **The 👶 panel** shows Blurp's card (#300): its level, five traits measured from its record (Curiosity, Insight, Adaptability, Focus, Sociability), what it is learning now, and a warning when an earned skill slips. The Skill tree tab (#296) draws the tree with each skill's test, score and bar.
  - **Toys** (#239, #267): built-in toys (body, fast, slow, tower, burst, die, drift and a ball) unlock as Blurp grows. You can poke them, it learns each one, and the dock says how well (#276). With the ball, Blurp learns to take turns with you in seconds (#278). A poke pulls its attention toward the toy, so it never freezes in a still world (#282).
  - **Membrane home shows it thinking** (#237, #250, #257): the amoeba reaches toward what it looks at, reacts to surprises, and turns toward you when it pushes. A level-up is celebrated with a chime and an optional notification, muted by 🔔 (#289).
  - **The Blurp zoo** (#283): one Home Assistant can hold several seats. Add them under *Configure*, switch with the 🦠 button or `select.membrane_seat`, and see every Blurp's card at a glance.
  - Its time accounting excludes walls (#305): running time with no built skill left to learn doesn't count as working hours.
  - Docs: [docs/selfgrowth.md](docs/selfgrowth.md) explains how to grow it, and [docs/sleep.md](docs/sleep.md) explains why it doesn't sleep yet.
- **A turn that repeats a refused call stops calling** (#274). If the same call is refused twice in one turn, the model is told to answer in plain text and its next round goes out without tools. A refused call with no tool name now gets the closest tool for its arguments suggested.
- **The tab icon and menu button are the babyseat** (#161).

### Fixes

- **The chat no longer shows "working" while the seat is idle** (#238): every turn now ends by reporting idle, and a busy page re-checks the seat every 5 s.
- **`membrane standalone` opens one browser tab, and none on a restart** (#243).
- **Curiosity no longer mistakes a restart for learning** (#259): a look that spans a gap in the record is not counted as progress or as evidence of a shift.
- **Test isolation** (#227, #287): the `/v1` hang test boots a private seat, and the Home Assistant detached test joins its loop thread before closing it.

## v0.6.4 (2026-10-19) — sharing that works

- **Security: imported entities can no longer carry code** (#226). "Imports take state, never code" was enforced only by the *publishing* seat — the importing side's strip removed nothing, so a modified or malicious seat writing to your shared Couchbase bucket could publish an entity whose getter/tick script your seat would store and run. Imports now strip code themselves, on first import and on every upgrade. Exposure needed write access to your bucket; upgrade if you share one.
- **A republished entity reaches the seats that already imported it** (#226): imports skipped any entity already present and ran only at boot, so updates never arrived. Now a newer published version upgrades the imported copy's state within about a minute (the presence pulse pulls, capped); a copy this seat edited keeps its edit and the divergence is recorded and announced (`diverged:`) once per version; copies imported before this release upgrade by timestamp. Presence rows are no longer imported (every seat's presence shares one id — a teammate's would have overwritten yours).
- **Publishing works on TLS-only clusters such as Couchbase Capella** (#225): the readiness probe always asked `http://<host>:8091`, so publish, presence and the peer roster never started on a `couchbases://` cluster. It now follows the scheme (`https://<host>:18091`, credentials only after the certificate verifies, a credential-less presence check otherwise, the TLS data port as a last resort), tries each host of a multi-host string, and still degrades quietly. The publish and peer connections now honour `COUCHBASE_CERT_PATH` too.
- **The team-sharing docs describe what sharing is** (#222): every seat is local-first; `publish` shares chosen entities (state, never code); presence and the peer roster come with a connected bucket; `MEMBRANE_FIELD_CLOUD=1` opts into the fully merged team field.

## v0.6.3 (2026-10-19) — the Ollama door opens again

- **Ollama-native clients get membrane, not the bare model** (#221): on the proxy port, `/api/chat` and `/api/generate` were forwarded to Ollama verbatim — no field, no doctrine, no capture, no tools — so a client speaking Ollama's API (Open WebUI in Ollama mode, an app's Ollama provider, Home Assistant's own Ollama integration) pointed at `:11500` got the plain model and nothing was remembered. **Every earlier release is affected**, from v0.2.0b1 through v0.6.2 (the Ollama path went with the old proxy in the September cleanup); OpenAI-style `/v1` clients — opencode, the web UI, the Membrane Home Assistant integration — were never affected. Both paths now run the same memory turn as `/v1/chat/completions` (gate, injection, capture, routing, JWT identity), answering in Ollama's shapes: buffered JSON with token counts, NDJSON streaming by default ending in a `done` record, tool calls translated both ways. `/api/tags`, `show`, `embed`, `pull` and Ollama's load/unload requests still pass straight through.

## v0.6.2 (2026-10-18) — the seat in a box

- **membrane in Docker** (#202): a public image, `chedim/membrane` on Docker Hub (amd64 + arm64), published by CI from each release. One `docker run` gives you the standalone seat: the web UI on :4096, the OpenAI-compatible proxy on :11500, and the memory on the `/data` volume. Configure it with `MEMBRANE_BACKEND`, `MEMBRANE_MODEL` and `MEMBRANE_EMBED_MODEL`. The image contains only the package: no state, no keys and no addresses are baked in. See [docs/docker.md](docs/docker.md). The README now covers Docker in Quick start and Installation, and fixes two stale claims: the proxy binds all interfaces by default, and re-embedding is `python -m membrane.reembed`.
- **JWT mode** (#203), optional and off by default. Set `MEMBRANE_JWT_SECRET` (or `MEMBRANE_JWT_SECRET_FILE`) and every request on both ports needs an HS256 token; `membrane token mint --sub ID --name NAME` issues them.
  - **Who is speaking:** the token's `sub` is who said a turn, so a client's own `[user: X]` line can no longer impersonate anyone.
  - **Sessions:** each session on :4096 belongs to its owner, and nobody else's are visible.
  - **Logging in:** the web UI asks for the token once and keeps it in an HttpOnly cookie, so it also works behind a reverse proxy. opencode and Home Assistant can pass the token as the Basic-auth password.
  - **Upstream and internal calls:** the token never reaches the model server, and the seat's own loopback calls carry an internal token.
  - See [docs/auth.md](docs/auth.md).

### Fixes

- **A template placeholder in the tool-name slot no longer burns the turn** (#182): after a restart the model could copy its chat template's example into the call's name (`{function_name}`, `<function-name>`), and membrane read any `{`-leading name as JSON arguments — every call bounced ("the call's NAME held its arguments"), and the refused call in history taught the model to repeat it. A placeholder name now counts as a missing name: the call is routed by what rides beside it (`fn_name`/`function_name`/… or the family owning the action), else refused naming the placeholder and the valid tools. The served tool manifest never carries a non-identifier name.
- **A loop reading an entity no longer wakes loops on it** (#188): a goal loop's read of a ticking getter (e.g. the eye's `beats` counter) posted `getter-applied` and `job-done` on the read entity, so every read woke another model pass there — passes that looked like a dropped field_watch probe that would not die. A loop's own read now answers the loop that asked; the field's own reads still post the change.
- **Background model calls are token-accounted** (#200): the thinker, the proxy-only watch degrade, the claim judge, the router's boot interview and `/delegate` spent tokens `membrane stats` never saw. Each now tallies its usage per lane and model into `<data dir>/bg-usage.json` (persisted across restarts), and `membrane stats` prints a `background model calls` section with its share of all tokens (`--json`: `background`).

## v0.6.1 (2026-10-15) — the field can talk

- **The field writes to people** (#154): `chat:out:<person>` is the twin of `voice:out` -- edit its `say` and the line lands in that person's latest chat (made on first use; `chat:out:operator` on a seat with no people). An entity's own script can now chat or speak with no model turn: a result `{'chat': '...', 'voice': '...', 'to': '<person>'}` is posted / spoken by the engine (`to` defaults to whoever chatted last). Guard rails: one line per 2 s per person; a script pushes at most once per 30 s and never repeats its last line.
- **A script set through `properties` goes through the script door** (#158): `entity_edit(properties={'tick_script': ...})` cut the script at 2000 chars and never compiled it, then answered ok. It is now compiled and capped (2048) like the `tick_script` argument, refused with the reason; any other value over 2000 chars is reported as truncated.
- **`presence.exec` returns the job's value** (#153): the tab ran the code as a function body, so a final expression like `1+1` came back `undefined`. The final expression, an explicit `return` and an awaited Promise all come back; values JSON can't carry are rendered readably.
- **No object reprs in job output** (#152): a getter returning its `me` handle stored the live helper object, and the refresh job printed `<..._Me object at 0x...>` on every read. Getter state is stored JSON-safe, and `membrane.me` reads as its entity id.

## v0.6.0 (2026-10-15) — the seat answers to you

- **The serve has no login of its own** (#144): membrane baked in a basic-auth pair (`opencode:<password>`, `serve-auth.json`) nobody asked for. It's gone — the serve, the `--serve-user`/`--serve-pass` flags and the pair file. membrane is a personal, self-hosted service: if you need a login, put a reverse proxy with one in front. Home Assistant keeps its own login; the integration waits on the seat being *ready* (it used to read "has a password" as ready), and connecting it to a seat needs no password.
- **Ctrl+C stops the seat, completely** (#145): `membrane standalone` / `serve` / `opencode` now *are* the seat process (exec), instead of a wrapper that SIGKILLed its seat child mid-teardown. `membrane stop` finds a seat that walked off its default ports and reads the seat's own pidfile.
- **A self-restart restarts** (#151): `seat::restart` sent the seat SIGTERM and waited for a supervisor; a foreground seat just stopped. It now re-execs after its teardown — same process, same terminal.
- **Goals survive a restart** (#147): the entity save dropped every entity's position, and the intent board lists only goals with one, so the board read "none held" after each boot. Positions are saved; a goal whose position was already lost is still held.
- **Failing scripts don't go silent** (#149): three strikes disabled a script for good. A struck-out script now retries on a backoff (60 s doubling to 1 h); a passing retry re-enables it; an entity read carries a failing script's state. Your own kill switch is never retried.
- **`entity_edit` says what it did with `properties`** (#150): it names the keys it set and refuses engine-owned / over-cap keys out loud, instead of answering rc 0 over a dropped patch.
- **`code=$(...)` is a shell assignment, not a program** (#148): the platform gate refused such lines rc 127.
- The doctrine tells seats to read back what they plant.

### Also in this release

- **`entity_read` reaches a ring-backed property's newest entries** (#105): voice keeps bounded rings (`voice:in:<person>.heard` / `voice:out:<person>.said`, 30 items of up to 600 chars) as entity properties, but the read-back clipped the whole-properties blob at ~800 chars, so only the first couple of items were visible and the newest entries — the tail an audit needs — were unreachable. `entity_read` now takes `prop=` to extract one property in full and `tail=N` to page a list-valued property from its newest end.

- **Refused tool calls no longer mint tool entities** (#110): a tool call the seat does not mount (the model's invented `f_1`..`f_4`) was refused by the surface but still captured as an observation, minting a live `tool:f_N` entity that rode the hot list — turn noise with no world referent. The live capture now drops unmounted tool names before they become observations (a `mounted_name` gate over the membrane families + legacy members, plus the client's own toolbelt), so a refused call leaves no live entity while real tools — membrane MCP and opencode built-ins alike — still capture.

- **`entity_add` takes `spec`, `fulfill` takes `intent`** (#108): the spec=/name= dialect split refused a value the model DID send — `entity_add(spec=X)` read only `name` so it answered "name required", and `fulfill(intent=176237)` / `fulfill(#N)` had no `intent` in its arg list so it answered "spec required" (even though `intend`'s own message says "fulfill #N closes it"). `entity_add` now accepts `spec` as the name (`name` still wins), and `fulfill` accepts `intent` (the `#N` / id form) as the spec.

- **`entity_query` sees the class-embedded files: pool** (#99, reported by Memo): a file entity minted by `entity_add` lands as `membrane::Component:files:x.py` (the class in the eid, the pool inside it), and the query read only the class as the kind — so `kind:file` returned nothing for those. The kind read now folds the eid through its canonical field id first, so `kind:file` sees the pool, while the entity still also matches `kind:component` (its class).

- **The graph's rows are now addressable** (#99, reported by Memo): the graph reader (`entities_related` / `graph`) returned FIELD ids (`f:x.py`), but the entity tools address the STORE's key — a file entity minted by `entity_add` lives as `membrane::Component:files:x.py` (the class-embedded pool spelling), which the field short-kind never reached. Each row (and its neighbours) now also carries the true stored `eid`, so whatever the graph returns is exactly what `entity_edit`/`entity_remove` can find.

- **`membrane erase-field` — the first-class memory wipe**: stop the seat, then move the field directory and the entity side-index out of the way (as `field.erased-<ts>` / `entities.json.erased-<ts>`, recoverable), instead of the operator hand-editing `mv` commands. `--yes` is mandatory; without it nothing is stopped or moved (rc 2). A clean wipe is a no-op success, and the receipt points at `membrane opencode` to boot fresh.

- **No phantom rebirth** (#99, reported by Memo): the last two symptoms — sibling file spellings never merged, and a mere prose mention of a dead path re-grew the garbage. A live, resolved file landing now absorbs its `~basename` phantom (the fallback spelling minted when the graph could not fold the path; mark-don't-delete), and the boot's track-entity guarantee no longer mints a store doc for a file track whose referent does not exist — the same stat-at-mint covenant the night's materialization already applies.

- **A dead file no longer mints an entity** (#99, reported by Memo): the file hands (`_file_land` and the read hand) minted a fresh `f:` entity for a failed op on a path that does not exist, so a deleted file kept earning graph-visible spellings whose getters error forever. The mint is now gated on existence — a failed op on a nonexistent path reports the canonical id but mints nothing (no getter to go stale); an entity that predates the file's death keeps its doc and records the error.

- **`membrane stats` — token/call accounting and the savings baseline**: reads a `serve-msgs` store (no model call) and prints sessions/turns/tokens (input, output, reasoning, cache-read), tool-call parts, tool-loop rounds, recall-injection size and models. `--baseline` compares the bounded replay window against a naive full-history replay, bucketed by conversation length — the numbers behind "does the seat actually save tokens" instead of a hunch.

- **`membrane bench` — the held-out recall benchmark, now re-runnable**: replays a seat's history into a fresh field, holds out a fraction of the mentions, and scores `recall()` against ground truth with the standard MRR + hit@k family, side by side across the `worldline` / `mean` / `state` reads. This is the script behind the worldlines MRR 0.210 → 0.539 number, shipped so the recall claim can be re-measured on any seat (against a field **copy**, via `membrane bench --field <copy>`).

- **The speaker lane plants goals instead of voicing them**: the voice-framed turn told the model to "say so" about tool use but never that a tool call is silent, so it narrated `goal(action='plant', …)` as inline code and that was spoken aloud. The frame now says a tool call is invisible and to make the call itself, and the streamer no longer speaks inline code.

## v0.5.0 (2026-10-15) — the seat gets experience

This release also brings v0.4.2's fixes to PyPI for the first time (see v0.4.2 below).

### Memory: the field learns from what happens
- **Entities are worldlines** (#65). An entity's life is kept as *episodes* (centroids of runs of 5 mentions), and recall reads a young entity by its mean and a mature one by its best episode, so it can find an entity through an old chapter of its life. Replaying a real seat's history and asking who 1,500 later, never-seen mentions were about: MRR 0.210 → **0.539** (mature entities 0.206 → 0.634). `recall(by="state")` keeps the old read; `MEMBRANE_WORLDLINES=0` restores it as the default. An upgraded field keeps its history (#70): until an entity's episodes cover its life, its mean is one more point, so an old entity is never read by its newest mentions alone.
- **Every change keeps its direction** (#55, #57; evidence in `experiments/delta_lane`, #56): beside how far a mention moved an entity, the field keeps *which way* (2048 sign bits, against the running state and against the previous mention).
- **"Changes like this one"** (#58): an entity's latest change and the past changes most like it, on itself and on others, recalled by resemblance.
- **Typed co-edges** (#62): a sentence naming two things records their relation's *kind* by resemblance, read with both things projected out, so the relation is measured rather than the pair. Edges written by the same sentence never answer each other.
- **Idioms** (#67): what the seat's acts are usually followed by (`grep → read`, `entity_add → entity_edit`, `python → git`), above chance only, on command and tool cards. Recording only: an idiom never runs anything.
- **Suggested methods** (#73): a shell idiom the seat keeps repeating on one thing (`grep -n … file` → `sed -n … file`) is offered as a ready two-step cliffhanger method, with a real example and the exact `exec(action='define', …)` to copy. Never defined by itself.
- **The recall indexes see new writes** (#64): "changes like this one" and edge recall froze at their first build on the file store, and rebuilt on every call on Couchbase.

### Forewarning (recording only: nothing reaches the model until the live calibration holds)
- **Moments** (#59): before a tool result exists, the outcomes that followed the most similar past moments. A real seat's history: AUC 0.65; flagged moments failed twice as often.
- **Acts** (#66): every tool call, forecast from its program's failure rate and the most similar earlier calls (the call itself, never its result). A real seat's history: AUC 0.768 on first-time calls; 71% of flagged calls went bad, against a 21% base rate.

### The viewer (#68)
The entity card shows its **life** (chapters, and its shape as percentiles among the field's grown entities: how far it jumps, how widely it ranges, how often it returns), "changes like this one", the sentence each edge was said in, and what a command is usually followed by. The HUD shows how many forewarned moments and calls went bad.
